# ADR 0007: Removing a bound Unix account (proposal, not implemented)

**Status:** Proposed; blocked on a proven migration from the pilot.11 broker state and a compatible per-start policy gate. Jari approved removal of his *test* mapped account in @deprovision-bound-user, not a general browser deletion feature or an unverified migration.

## Why a ledger edit and `userdel -r` are unsafe

The v1 binding ledger records live bindings only. The writer chooses an unused UID from its configured pool, so removing a binding makes its UID eligible again. Keeping the binding and deleting its Unix account instead makes broker startup and routing fail verification. The Fleetrepo per-start gate also expects every binding to have a live account and an exact version-1 ledger. Deletion is therefore a new state transition shared by the root broker and the root-owned activation policy, not a standalone Linux command.

The pilot.11 `broker-high-water.json` was written at initial seeding only; the broker does not advance it on new bindings. `head.json` remembers the largest *generation*, not which UID or subject was ever assigned. A snapshot of today's four state files and NSS can show current assignments, but cannot prove that an absent UID was never assigned and later removed by an operator. This is a migration prerequisite, not permission to infer history from free slots. A matching original seed receipt, an independently verified account history and a complete, authenticated binding history (or an operator decision to quarantine every unprovable slot) are needed before admitting v1 into a no-reuse format. An unverified restore must fail closed. The operator's private inventory and receipts belong in Fleetrepo, not this public repository.

## Proposed state and authority

Keep this operation exclusively local and root-controlled. The gateway's broker socket is accessible to the gateway UID for `ensure`/`authorize`; it must **not** accept deletion or select a target Unix UID from a browser request. A root-only operator entry point should take a canonical numeric provider subject, resolve the exact committed binding under the broker directory lock, and refuse a missing/ambiguous binding or any pending transaction. No wildcard or arbitrary username/home argument. The root broker owns durable mutation; the gateway owns in-memory session/OAuth revocation; Fleetrepo owns guest activation and the private migration proof.

Use one versioned root-only ledger with distinct live and retired records. A retired record permanently reserves the original UID/GID and name and identifies the original subject, with a monotonically advancing generation. A subject may have a new live binding only at a **different** UID; a retired name may not be silently reclaimed, even by the same subject. A new login with no approved, free name/UID returns a typed refusal requiring an operator alternate or explicit exhaustion response. Reject duplicate subjects among live rows, overlapping retired/live names or UIDs, missing records, counter rollback, unknown versions, or extra fields. The head and a durable independently protected high-water commitment must cover the retired set; version numbers or counters alone do not prove no rollback. The state format and migration are not selected until the host policy has an equivalent parser and recovery/rollback contract. A rollback of just the product release after migrating state must refuse startup, never reinterpret retired records as live v1.

Before changing NSS or unlinking any path, write and fsync an immutable root-only journal identifying the subject and committed binding, phase, generation and exact next operation while holding the broker's exclusive state lock. Its existence denies all broker routes and per-start activation. Stop new subject work and invalidate old sessions and pre-existing OAuth callbacks using the gateway's root-only control channel **while the binding is still valid and routable**; this control currently calls broker `authorize`, so holding the exclusive lock during that call would deadlock. After acknowledgment, take the lock and revalidate the binding. The gap admits new login attempts: the protocol must either add an admission freeze before revocation with a narrowly scoped gateway control that does not rely on `authorize`, or explicitly prove that a later journal and recheck deny every new route and invalidate any session issued during the gap. The current revocation cutoff alone is insufficient: OAuth states begun after that cutoff can produce a new session. No removal may start before this ordering is proved.

The journal remains on disk through drain and deletion. Stop the exact mapped socket and service; prove no subject processes, foreign FDs into the home, mounts or other users' dependencies remain. Verify each target through descriptor-relative, no-follow ownership and inode checks against the journal, never derive deletion paths from an untrusted username. Delete only the bound user's scoped config, runtime socket/directory and home, remove its named shared-parent ACL entries without modifying siblings, then remove its exact passwd and private group records with checked NSS state. A stopped or unresponsive process, incomplete scan, unknown mount, changed inode, or failure at any phase leaves the marker and service unavailable for investigation. A retry must inspect phase and actual filesystem/NSS state, not simply repeat `userdel -r`; that tool's recursive traversal and partial results cannot establish this contract. Record the retired binding durably and verify absence before clearing the journal. A phase that is interrupted between OS mutation and journal advancement needs a specific idempotent reconciliation rule; no generic rollback recreates a deleted home. Only after a committed tombstone and successful verification may the sibling routes resume.

This is **not** atomic with already executing HTTP requests or native agents. Gateway per-request broker checks protect later requests, but a request that passed before freeze may still have open descriptors. The operator must quiesce and prove this boundary rather than promise immediate removal of already-running work. Credential grants held by the provider are not revoked by account deletion. Test with two synthetic UIDs and fail injection at every fsync, stop, scan, unlink, NSS and publication step, plus concurrent ensures, old cookies/OAuth state, new login, and power-cycle startup; a unit test is not evidence of the live guest.

## Blocker before implementation or live action

The current product and Fleetrepo policy both require the exact v1 ledger and have no durable retired set. The only external high-water receipt is initial, not updated per assignment; it cannot authenticate absent historical UIDs. Fleetrepo's gate currently parses exactly `{"version":1,"generation":...,"bindings":...}` and insists all bindings resolve to NSS. No product-only change can make it accept tombstones, and a raw guest-side deletion would cause startup/activation drift. Agree a migration proof, choose a tombstone/high-water commitment and a jointly staged Fleetrepo gate/release rollback before enabling any deletion command. Until then **do not delete a bound account, remove its ledger row or free its UID**. A root operator may still use the existing session revoke without changing the account.
