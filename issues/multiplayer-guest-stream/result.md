# Result: participant history read, filtered live stream and controls

Implemented ADR 0038 sections 6 and 8 behind the experimental feature `multiplayer`. A joined participant can now read one conversation, follow it live, write, take their own held message back, stop a turn and say they are present, through `/api/v1/shared/<share_id>/<operation>`. No browser UI (that is `multiplayer-browser`).

## What exists now

- **Guest table** (`internal/daemon/guest_linux.go`, handlers in `guest_routes_linux.go`): `invite` (GET; the only read an invited person may make), `join`, `state`, `history`, `blocks/<entry>/<index>`, `events`, `send`, `cancel`, `abort`, `presence`. Every handler checks the record (`authorizeGuest`, or `admitGuestStream` for the stream) before reading any input; every refusal is `resource_not_found`.
- **History read**: the owner's history reader in a guest mode (`historyHandlerFor(..., guest)` in `history_linux.go`) on a path the daemon builds from the admitted ID; only the query crosses. It gives thinking, tool calls with their arguments and results, participant messages and Habitat lines exactly as the owner reads them, and leaves out the owner's `!!` commands (excluded from the agent's context, so not part of the conversation), with page cursors that step over them, and the owner's process-run (taskfleet) details. Block reads of a hidden entry are refused. Recap and spinoff routes are not reachable.
- **Typed facts in history** (`shared_facts_linux.go`, owner and guest alike, also in `history.entry` events): `participant_message` `{message_id, subject, display_name, role, text}` (the participant's own words without Habitat's opening lines) and `conversation_line` `{line_id, kind, event|mode|request, subject, display_name}`. Details that the record could not have written give no author.
- **Daemon facts** read from the whole active branch, porting the companion's `ownerInputs`/`turnModeFromBranch`/`waitingRequests`: the latest run decision (`turn`: mode, who started it, request), the waiting requests (oldest first, with entry key), and the newest release with its per-call answers from `oh-approval` entries. Participants get them in `state`; the owner on a new owner route `GET /api/v1/conversations/<id>/shared` (gateway allowlist updated, GET only). `state` also gives the run state (running/idle/starting/stopping/stopped, aborting), the pending question's kind only, the participant's own held messages, the joined members, presence, the title, the workspace's display name only, and a `journal_cursor` with the running turn's `live_tail` taken together.
- **Stream** `GET /guest/v1/conversations/<id>/events?after=`: the owner's journal through `guestFrame`: only `resource == {agent_id: pi:<id>}`, only the allowlisted kinds (message and thinking deltas, tool-call and tool rows, settles, Pi lifecycle/markers/retries, presence, invalidations, history entries). `question.requested` becomes a bare invalidation; `queue.changed`, `command.changed`, `bash.output`, `process.*`, `extension.visible`, `notice.created` and every new kind are dropped. A `!!` history entry is dropped and process-run details are stripped. The owner's whole-daemon invalidations pass (they say only that something changed). Removal and archive end the stream (admission under `memberMu`), and the record is rechecked at least every heartbeat interval, also while the stream replays (`eventJournal.serve` gained a filter and an `alive` check).
- **Controls**: `send` holds through `holdParticipantMessage`; `cancel` takes back only one's own held message; `abort` stops a running turn whoever started it, without touching the owner's command receipts (Pi's abort also ends an open approval question, because the companion asks with the run's signal); `stop` and every other control stay owner-only.
- **Presence** in daemon memory only: `present` lasts 60 s, `typing` 8 s, `left` ends it; changes are published as a new journal kind `presence.changed` to the owner's and participants' browsers; removal and archive forget it. Lock order `memberMu` → `presenceMu`.
- **Gateway** (`internal/gateway/shares_linux.go`): the allowlist holds all ten operations by method; `readGuestCall` reads each operation's query and body against its grammar and rebuilds them (keys, patterns, single values; JSON bodies decoded strictly and re-encoded). Finite replies pass through bounded (4 MiB, 70 s for history/blocks/state; 64 KiB, 15 s otherwise) and only as JSON; refusals keep the daemon's code. `events` is relayed as a stream tied to the browser session and the share (removal and withdrawal cancel it).
- **Contracts**: new `conversation-guest.schema.json` (state root; invite, send, cancel, abort, presence, presence event payload, owner facts), `history.schema.json` `participantMessage`/`conversationLine`, `event.schema.json` kind `presence.changed` and the typed history-entry fields; example `conversation-guest.json`.

## Decisions for later review

1. **Tool-call arguments are visible to participants.** ADR §6: history "as the owner's history read gives them"; §5's owner-only detail is the approval question, which participants never get (only its kind). Alternative: strip arguments from tool frames and refuse toolCall block reads for guests (small change in `guestFrame` and the guest reader). The comment in `pi_approval_linux.go` that called arguments owner-only was corrected.
2. **The invite card's title** (carry-over from routing) is served here by `GET .../invite`, and is only the conversation's native name, empty when it has none. Alternative: the rail's title, whose fallback is the first message — conversation text before joining. The browser shows a generic title for an empty one.
3. **The owner's `!!` commands are hidden** from participants (history, blocks, stream); `!` commands in the agent's context are shown when done, not their live output. Alternative: show both; but `!!` is explicitly kept out of the conversation.
4. **The owner's process-run details** (taskfleet spinoff state on process cards) are stripped for participants; they are the owner's control surface, like view cards. View cards need no daemon change: the guest table has no view route; the browser hides the open action.
5. **Turn mode, waiting requests and answers are state facts, not new journal kinds.** Their changes arrive as `history.entry`, `pi.marker` (`entry_appended`), invalidations and settles, after which the browser rereads `state`. Only presence got a kind, because rereading for every typing change would be wasteful. Alternative: kinds for each.
6. **Facts are read from the whole active branch per `state` read**, because an old request can still wait; only conversations in the record are read. No cache (pre-release, no store without a need); the read gets the history read's 60 s write deadline.
7. **Participants see only their own held messages**, not others' (nor the owner's). The owner's presence is not tracked for participants.
8. **The conversation ID is visible to a joined participant** (in `agent_id`, history and events). It grants nothing: routes go by share ID and the record.
9. **A participant's abort writes no Habitat line** (who stopped is logged by the daemon without the subject). Alternative: a line "X stopped the turn" via the companion.
10. **Feature off** ends guest streams within one heartbeat interval (20 s) rather than at once: it is a preference write, which cancels nothing.

## Review

Critical rigor (cross-user read/act boundary, contract, daemon concurrency). `/llm-review`: gpt-6-astra finder, claude-fable-5-2 verifier, two calls; `/assess-findings`: four FIX (all applied with tests), one DISCUSS (decision 1). Fixed: the stream rechecked the record only when the journal went quiet (`TestGuestStreamRechecksWhileReplaying`); whole-branch fact reads under the 5 s write deadline; the invite title fell back to the first message; the invite picker's small-bounded members read would have carried the facts (moved to the owner's `shared` route). Also fixed a low-rate test flake in the predecessor's `newGuestTurnFixture` (a background line delivery could rewrite the record after the test ended).
